### User Research Methods: Why Direct Data Collection Still Leads the Way

> In an ideal development cycle, the primary sources of information should be direct user
> interviews and structured observation sessions, as these methods provide first-hand insight
> into actual user behavior rather than assumed needs. That said, complementary sources such
> as domain experts, supervisors, customer support teams, and surveys also play a valid role
> in filling knowledge gaps, particularly when direct user access is limited during early
> project stages. The reason interviews and observation remain the most valuable techniques
> is that they allow the team to cross-reference what users say they want with what they
> actually do, which is a critical distinction in software development since perceived needs
> and real usage patterns often diverge significantly [1]. This comparison is what enables
> the team to prioritize features by actual impact rather than by assumption, and to discard
> functionality that would consume development resources without delivering real value.
> The effectiveness of both methods can be further improved by designing them around a
> well-defined user persona, as having a specific target profile allows the team to structure
> questions and observation criteria that lead directly to actionable and measurable
> requirements [2].

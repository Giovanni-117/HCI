### Lean UX in Practice: Adapting Agile Principles to Real Software Projects

> Lean UX introduces a shift in how development teams approach design, moving away from 
> heavy documentation and rigid requirements toward problem statements, assumptions, hypotheses,
> minimum viable products, and "quick and dirty" research as its core building blocks [1].
> While not every one of these elements can be directly applied to every project, techniques
> like MVP and quick and dirty research remain highly adaptable, since they are lightweight
> enough to integrate into existing workflows without major disruption. The more collaborative
> and research-intensive aspects of the methodology may present challenges depending on team
> size or time constraints, but they can still be scaled down to fit the project's scope.
> The key adjustment would be shortening certain activities to keep them viable, and then
> running user validation tests to determine whether each one is actually delivering value.
> This last step is critical, as Lean UX is fundamentally driven by continuous feedback loops
> rather than assumptions, meaning that any adapted activity must be measured against real
> user behavior to justify its place in the process [2].
